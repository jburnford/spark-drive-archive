Broom Corn

the *Sorghum dora*, which is cultivated for its brush to make brooms; the grain of the panicles forms excellent food for poultry. In many of the United States of America it is extensively grown—the annual produce of the plant, in the State of New York, is valued at one million sterling. [P. L. Simmonds, The Commercial Dictionary of Trade Products (1892), scan p. 64]
