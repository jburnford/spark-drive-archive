Bark

The outer rind of trees. There is an immense variety of barks known in commerce as cinnamon, Peruvian bark, oak bark, quercitron &c. The term *bark* is, however, generally employed to express either Peruvian bark, or oak bark and quercitron, and it is these only we here notice. Peruvian bark (Fr. quinquina; Ger. kronchina; Dutch, china-bast; Span. quina, quenina; Lat. quenina, Cortex Peruvianus). There are several sorts of this bark known in commerce. The first sort is the *pale* bark of the shops. It is the original cinchona of Peru, and is the produce of different varieties of the *Chinchona officinalis* of Linnaeus. [J. R. McCulloch, Dictionary of Commerce and Commercial Navigation (1880), scan p. 195]
