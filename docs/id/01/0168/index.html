Starch

(_Amidon_, _Fecule_, Fr.; _Stärke_, Germ.); is a white pulverulent substance, composed of microscopic spheroids, which are bags containing the amylaceous matter. It exists in a great many different plants, and varies merely in the form and size of its microscopic particles; as found in some plants, it consists of spherical particles 1/1000 of an inch in diameter; and in others, of ovoid particles, of 1/300 or 1/400 of an inch. It occurs, 1. in the seeds of all the acotyledinous plants, among which are the several species of corns, and those of other _gramineæ_; 2. in the round perennial tap roots, which shoot up an annual stem; [Andrew Ure, A Dictionary of Arts, Manufactures, and Mines (Project Gutenberg #44562)]
