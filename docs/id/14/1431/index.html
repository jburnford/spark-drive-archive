Verdegris

(GER. *Grünspan*. DU. *Spaansch groen*. DA. *Spansk grønt*. SW. *Spank gröna*. FR. *Verde-gris*, *Verdet*. IT. *Verderame*. SP. *Cardenillo*, *Verdete*, *Verde-gris*. PORT. *Verdete*, *Verdegris*, *Cardinillo*. RUS. *Jar*. POL. *Gryszpan*. LAT. *Ærugo*, *Viride æris*,) is an acetat of copper, useful in the arts as a pigment. The principles on which it is formed are these : Acetic acid attacks copper very slowly in open vessels, converts it into an oxide, and dissolves it; but in close vessels no action takes place. This acid readily combines with the oxide of copper, and forms with it an acetat. This salt was known by the ancients; and various ways of preparing it are described by Pliny. It is usually obtained by exposing plates of copper to the action of vinegar, till they are converted to a blueish green powder, and then dissolving this powder in acetic acid, and crystallising it. Acetat of copper crystallises in four-sided truncated pyramids. [Thomas Mortimer (rev. Dickinson), A General Commercial Dictionary (1823), scan p. 980]
